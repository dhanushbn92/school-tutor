Explore the airway

Air travels: nostrils → pharynx → larynx → trachea → bronchi → bronchioles → alveoli, where gas exchange occurs. Tap any labelled part.

Breathing mechanics & transport
  • Inhalation: diaphragm contracts & flattens, ribs rise — thoracic volume ↑, pressure ↓, air rushes in.
  • Exhalation: diaphragm relaxes & domes up, ribs fall — volume ↓, pressure ↑, air pushed out.
  • O₂ transport: ~97% carried by haemoglobin as oxyhaemoglobin in RBCs.
  • CO₂ transport: ~70% as bicarbonate (HCO₃⁻) in plasma, ~23% as carbamino-haemoglobin, ~7% dissolved.

Excretory System & the Nephron

Kidney & nephron

Each kidney has ~1 million nephrons — the functional units that filter blood and form urine. Switch to Nephron zoom, then run the animation to watch filtration, reabsorption and secretion.

Urine formation — three stages
  • 1. Glomerular filtration: high pressure in the glomerulus forces water, glucose, salts, urea & amino acids out of blood into Bowman's capsule (filtrate). Blood cells & proteins stay behind.
  • 2. Selective reabsorption: in PCT, loop & DCT, useful materials (all glucose, most water, ions) return to blood capillaries.
  • 3. Tubular secretion: extra H⁺, K⁺, ammonia & drugs are actively added from blood into the tubule — final urine flows via collecting duct → ureter → bladder.

Nitrogenous Wastes & Water Availability

Proteins & nucleic acids break down to toxic ammonia. How an animal disposes of it depends on how much water it can afford to lose. Tap a card.

Why the difference?

Ammonia is very toxic and needs lots of water to flush out; urea is far less toxic; uric acid is almost non-toxic and nearly insoluble, so it is excreted as a paste with minimal water loss — ideal where water is scarce.
