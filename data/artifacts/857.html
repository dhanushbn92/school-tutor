The three lines of defence
  • 1st — Barriers (innate, non-specific): skin, mucus, cilia, tears & saliva (lysozyme), and stomach acid (pH ~1.5–2) keep most microbes out.
  • 2nd — Innate cellular: if breached, phagocytes (neutrophils, macrophages) engulf microbes; inflammation (redness, heat, swelling) and fever slow spread. Fast but non-specific, no memory.
  • 3rd — Adaptive (specific): lymphocytes (B & T cells) recognise a specific antigen, mount a tailored attack, and form memory cells. Slower first time, but remembers.
Innate
Present from birth · fast (mins–hrs) · non-specific · no memory
Adaptive
Develops after exposure · slower (days) · highly specific · has memory

The Immune Response — humoral & cell-mediated

Two arms of adaptive immunity
  • Humoral (antibody-mediated): B-cells activate → become plasma cells that secrete antibodies (immunoglobulins). Antibodies neutralise/agglutinate free antigens & toxins in blood & lymph.
  • Cell-mediated: Helper T-cells (TH) coordinate the response; Cytotoxic T-cells (TC) kill infected/cancerous cells directly.
  • Memory cells persist after the infection clears → the basis of long-term immunity.
Primary vs Secondary response
Second exposure: memory cells give a faster, larger, longer-lasting antibody response.

Vaccination & Types of Immunity

A vaccine carries harmless antigen (killed/attenuated microbe, or a protein/mRNA). It trains B & T cells and builds memory — without causing disease.

When immunity misfires
  • Allergy (hypersensitivity): over-reaction to a harmless allergen (pollen, dust) — IgE & histamine cause sneezing, itching; severe cases → anaphylaxis.
  • Autoimmunity: immune system attacks the body's own cells — e.g. rheumatoid arthritis, type-1 diabetes.
