Metallurgy: Ore to Pure Metal

The main steps of extracting a metal from its ore.

1. Ore miningDig ore from the earth's crust (e.g. haematite for iron).
2. ConcentrationRemove gangue (impurities) — e.g. froth flotation, washing.
3. ReductionConvert ore to metal — roasting/calcination then reduction (e.g. with carbon in a blast furnace).
4. RefiningPurify the metal, e.g. electrolytic refining.

Reactivity guides the method: highly reactive metals (K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al) are extracted by electrolysis; moderately reactive (Zn, Fe, Pb) by reduction with carbon; least reactive (Ag, Au) often occur native (free) in nature.

Corrosion: Rusting of Iron

Iron rusts (forms hydrated iron(III) oxide, Fe₂O₃·xH₂O) only when both air (oxygen) and water are present. Toggle the conditions:

Prevention: galvanising (coating with zinc), painting, greasing/oiling, and electroplating all keep air & water away from the iron surface.

Quick Facts

  • Metals are usually lustrous, malleable, ductile, sonorous and good conductors of heat & electricity.
  • Non-metals are generally dull, brittle (if solid) and poor conductors — but graphite (carbon) conducts electricity.
  • Mercury (Hg) is the only metal liquid at room temperature; bromine is the only liquid non-metal.
  • Sodium & potassium are so soft they can be cut with a knife and are stored under kerosene.
  • Metal oxides are basic; non-metal oxides are acidic.
  • Aluminium is highly reactive yet resists corrosion because a thin oxide layer protects it.
