3. Law of Constant (Definite) Proportions

A pure compound always contains the same elements in the same fixed proportion by mass, no matter the source or amount. Water is always H : O = 1 : 8 by mass.

Hydrogen (2 × 1)
—
Oxygen (1 × 16)

Mass ratio H : O
1 : 8

In H2O the mass of H is 2×1=2 and O is 1×16=16, so H:O = 2:16 = 1:8. Whatever the total mass, this ratio never changes — that is the law of constant proportions.

Key facts

Atom
The smallest particle of an element that takes part in a chemical reaction. Example: one O atom.
Molecule
Two or more atoms chemically bonded together. Can be of an element (O2) or a compound (H2O).
Valency
The combining capacity of an element = number of bonds it forms. H=1, O=2, N=3, C=4, Na=1, Cl=1.
Mole (mol)
The amount of substance containing 6.022 × 10²³ particles. Mass of 1 mole in grams = molar mass.
Avogadro's number (Nₐ)
6.022 × 10²³ — the number of particles in one mole.
