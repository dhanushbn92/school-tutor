The Human Skeleton

206 bones grouped into the axial skeleton (skull, vertebral column, ribs, sternum — 80 bones) and the appendicular skeleton (limbs and their girdles — 126 bones). Tap any part to learn more, or pick a joint type above to watch it move.

  • Bones store calcium & phosphorus and house red marrow that makes blood cells.
  • A joint (articulation) is where two or more bones meet; freely movable ones are synovial joints.
  • Ligaments join bone to bone; tendons attach muscle to bone.

Sliding-filament theory

How a muscle shortens

A nerve impulse releases Ca2+ from the sarcoplasmic reticulum. Ca2+ binds troponin, exposing binding sites on actin. Myosin heads form cross-bridges and, powered by ATP, pull the thin filaments toward the centre. The Z-lines are drawn closer and the sarcomere shortens — the filaments themselves do not shrink.

  • Sarcomere = functional unit, from one Z-line to the next.
  • On contraction the H-zone and I-band narrow; the A-band stays constant.
  • ATP is needed both to power the power-stroke and to detach myosin from actin (its absence causes rigor).
  • Antagonistic pair: when the biceps contracts the arm flexes; when the triceps contracts it extends.
