Two coherent slits make waves overlap. Bright fringes fall where the path difference is a whole number of wavelengths (path diff = mλ); dark fringes where it is a half-odd multiple. Spacing β = λD/d — widen the slits and fringes crowd together.

A single slit spreads light into a broad, bright central maximum flanked by fainter side maxima. Dark bands (minima) sit where a sinθ = mλ (m = ±1, ±2, …). Narrow the slit and the pattern fans out wider.

Unpolarized light loses half its intensity through the first polarizer, becoming plane-polarized. The analyzer passes only the component along its axis, so I = I₀cos²θ. Crossed polarizers (θ = 90°) give darkness.

Quick quiz — tap an answer

1. In Young's experiment, if the slit separation d is increased, the fringe spacing β…

Correct: decreases. Since β = λD/d, β is inversely proportional to d — larger d packs fringes closer.

2. In single-slit diffraction, dark fringes (minima) occur when…

Correct: a sinθ = mλ. Note m = 0 is the central maximum, not a minimum, so minima start at m = ±1.

3. Plane-polarized light hits an analyzer turned to θ = 60°. The transmitted intensity is…

Correct: I₀/4. Malus's law: I = I₀cos²60° = I₀(0.5)² = I₀/4.
