From the surface of radius r: v_orbit = √(GM/r), v_escape = √(2GM/r) = √2 · v_orbit. If speed < v_orbit the craft falls back (sub-orbital); between v_orbit and v_escape it circles/ellipses in a bound orbit; at or above v_escape it leaves for good.

Quick Check (3 MCQs)

1. As a satellite is moved to a higher circular orbit, its orbital speed v₀ = √(GM/r):

Correct: decreases. v₀ = √(GM/r), so larger r means smaller orbital speed (though the period increases as T² ∝ r³).

2. Where is the acceleration due to gravity g the greatest?

Correct: at the surface. Going up, g′ = g(R/(R+h))² falls off; going down, g′ = g(1−d/R) also falls, reaching 0 at the centre. The surface is the maximum.

3. Escape velocity from a planet's surface is related to orbital velocity by:

Correct: v_e = √2 · v₀. Since v_e = √(2GM/r) and v₀ = √(GM/r), their ratio is exactly √2 ≈ 1.414 (e.g. Earth: 7.9 → 11.2 km/s).
