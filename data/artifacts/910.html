Angle of Elevation

An observer on the ground looks UP at the top of a tower. The line of sight makes the angle of elevation θ with the horizontal. Height = distance × tan θ.

0.577
Height of tower

In right triangle: tan θ = opposite / adjacent = height / distance
height = d · tan θ = 30 · tan 30° = 17.32 m

Angle of Depression

An observer at the top of a cliff of height h looks DOWN at a boat. The angle of depression α (below the horizontal) equals the angle of elevation from the boat — they are alternate angles across the horizontal parallels.

tan α
1.000
Horizontal distance
40.00 m
Line of sight
56.57 m
Angle of depression = angle of elevation from the boat (alternate angles).
tan α = h / distance ⟹ distance = h / tan α = 40 / tan 45° = 40.00 m

Key Facts

  • Line of sight: the straight line from the observer's eye to the object being viewed.
  • Angle of elevation: the angle the line of sight makes with the horizontal when the object is above the observer (looking up).
  • Angle of depression: the angle below the horizontal when the object is below the observer (looking down). It equals the angle of elevation from the object (alternate angles).
  • Choosing the ratio: use tan when height & horizontal distance are involved; sin with the line of sight (hypotenuse) & opposite side; cos with hypotenuse & adjacent side.
  • Special angles: tan 30° = 1/√3 ≈ 0.577, tan 45° = 1, tan 60° = √3 ≈ 1.732.
