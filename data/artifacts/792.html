Formulas & Quiz

Doping a Silicon Lattice

Free electron (–) Hole (+) Donor ion (P) Acceptor ion (B)
  • Intrinsic: pure Si — equal, small numbers of thermally generated electrons and holes (ne = nh = ni).
  • n-type: pentavalent donor (P, As) gives an extra loosely-bound electron. Majority = electrons, minority = holes.
  • p-type: trivalent acceptor (B, Al) leaves a vacancy (hole). Majority = holes, minority = electrons.
  • The doped crystal stays electrically neutral overall.

p–n Junction Diode & Biasing

Electrons Holes Depletion region
  • Depletion region: near the junction, electrons and holes recombine, leaving fixed ions and a built-in potential barrier.
  • Forward bias (p to +, n to –): barrier shrinks; once V > knee (~0.7 V for Si), current rises steeply.
  • Reverse bias (p to –, n to +): barrier widens; only a tiny saturation leakage current flows.
  • A diode therefore conducts in one direction — it acts as a rectifier.

Energy Band Diagrams

Conduction band Valence band Forbidden gap (Eg)
  • Conductor (e.g. Cu): valence and conduction bands overlap — Eg ≈ 0. Electrons move freely.
  • Semiconductor (Si Eg ≈ 1.1 eV, Ge ≈ 0.7 eV): small gap; some electrons cross at room temperature.
  • Insulator (e.g. diamond Eg ≈ 6 eV): large gap; almost no electrons reach the conduction band.
  • Higher temperature or doping increases conduction-band electrons in a semiconductor.

Formulas & Key Facts

ne · nh = ni2  (mass-action law)
Band gap: Eg(Si) ≈ 1.1 eV, Eg(Ge) ≈ 0.7 eV
Knee (cut-in) voltage: VSi ≈ 0.7 V, VGe ≈ 0.3 V
Diode current: I = I0(eeV/kT − 1)
  • Forward bias: p-side to +, n-side to −. Low resistance, large current above knee voltage.
  • Reverse bias: p-side to −, n-side to +. High resistance, tiny reverse saturation current I0.
  • Rectifier: because a diode conducts in only one direction, it converts AC to pulsating DC (half-wave / full-wave).
  • Majority carriers: electrons in n-type, holes in p-type. Minority carriers are the opposite type.
  • Doping level is tiny (~1 part in 106) yet hugely raises conductivity.
