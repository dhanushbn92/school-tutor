Diffusion is the spreading of particles from high to low concentration due to their constant random motion. Warmer particles move faster, so diffusion is quicker at higher temperature.

Key facts

  • Solid: particles packed in a fixed lattice, only vibrate; fixed shape & volume.
  • Liquid: particles close but mobile; fixed volume, takes shape of container.
  • Gas: particles far apart, fast, random; no fixed shape or volume, highly compressible.
  • Kinetic energy & temperature: average particle KE rises with temperature (in kelvin). Hotter = faster particles.
  • Latent heat: heat absorbed or released during a change of state without a temperature change. Latent heat of fusion (melting/freezing); latent heat of vaporisation (boiling/condensing).
  • Kelvin scale: T(K) = T(°C) + 273. Melting point of ice = 273 K, boiling point of water = 373 K.

Quick quiz (tap an answer)

1. During melting or boiling the temperature of a substance stays constant. The heat supplied is used as…

Correct: the supplied heat is latent heat — it overcomes forces between particles to change state, so the thermometer reading does not rise.

2. The direct change of a solid to a gas without passing through the liquid state is called…

Correct: sublimation, e.g. camphor or dry ice (solid CO₂) turning straight into vapour.

3. Compared to a solid, particles in a gas have…

Correct: gas particles move fast (high KE) and are widely spaced with negligible forces between them.
