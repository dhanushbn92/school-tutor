The DNA Double Helix

Click any base to inspect it. Two antiparallel strands (5′→3′ and 3′→5′) held by a sugar-phosphate backbone; bases pair by hydrogen bonds: A=T (2 bonds), G≡C (3 bonds).

A — Adenine T — Thymine G — Guanine C — Cytosine
Base info: click a base above to see its pairing partner and details.
DNA vs RNA: DNA is double-stranded, uses deoxyribose sugar and the base Thymine (T). RNA is usually single-stranded, uses ribose sugar and Uracil (U) in place of T. Adenine pairs with T in DNA but with U in RNA.

Central Dogma: DNA → RNA → Protein

Step: pick a process above to animate it.
Replication is semiconservative: each daughter DNA keeps one old (template) strand and one newly made strand. Transcription copies a gene into mRNA using base pairing, with U replacing T. Translation reads mRNA in triplets (codons); tRNA anticodons deliver amino acids at the ribosome to build a polypeptide.

Types of effect: Silent new codon codes the same amino acid · Missense a different amino acid · Nonsense a stop codon appears early · Frameshift insertion/deletion (not a multiple of 3) shifts the whole reading frame.
Example: in sickle-cell anaemia a single substitution (GAG→GUG) changes glutamic acid to valine — a missense mutation.
