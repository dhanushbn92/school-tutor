Key facts

Slope of a line
The slope m = tanθ, where θ is the angle the line makes with the positive x-axis. Through two points, m = (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁). A horizontal line has m = 0; a vertical line has undefined slope (θ = 90°).
The five forms of a line
Slope-intercept: y = mx + c.   Point-slope: y − y₁ = m(x − x₁).   Two-point: (y − y₁)/(y₂ − y₁) = (x − x₁)/(x₂ − x₁).   Intercept: x/a + y/b = 1 (a, b are x- and y-intercepts).   General: ax + by + c = 0, with slope −a/b.
Angle between two lines
If lines have slopes m₁ and m₂, then tanθ = |(m₁ − m₂)/(1 + m₁m₂)|. The acute angle is taken. Parallel: m₁ = m₂ (θ = 0). Perpendicular: m₁·m₂ = −1 (θ = 90°, denominator 1 + m₁m₂ = 0).
Distance from a point to a line
For line ax + by + c = 0 and point (x₀, y₀): distance = |ax₀ + by₀ + c| / √(a² + b²). The perpendicular from the point meets the line at the foot of the perpendicular.
Distance between two parallel lines
Parallel lines y = mx + c₁ and y = mx + c₂ are distance |c₁ − c₂| / √(1 + m²) apart. In general form ax + by + c₁ = 0 and ax + by + c₂ = 0, it is |c₁ − c₂| / √(a² + b²).
