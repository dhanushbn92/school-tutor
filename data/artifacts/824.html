Key facts

Ecosystem
A community of living organisms (biotic) interacting with their non-living surroundings (abiotic: air, water, soil, sunlight) as one working unit.
Producers (autotrophs)
Green plants & algae make their own food by photosynthesis. They form the first trophic level and support every other organism.
Consumers (heterotrophs)
Cannot make food. Herbivores (primary) eat producers; carnivores (secondary/tertiary) eat other consumers; omnivores eat both.
Decomposers
Bacteria & fungi (saprotrophs) break down dead plants, animals and waste, returning nutrients to the soil and air.
10% energy law (Lindeman)
Only about 10% of the energy at one trophic level passes to the next; ~90% is lost as heat and metabolism. This limits food chains to ~4-5 links.
Food web
Many interlinked food chains. Webs are more stable than single chains because organisms have alternative food sources.
Biogeochemical cycles
Carbon, nitrogen and water cycle continuously between organisms and the environment, so a fixed amount of matter is reused forever.
