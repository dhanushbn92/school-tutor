The Primary Rainbow

A ray hits a spherical water drop: it refracts on entry, undergoes one total-ish internal reflection at the back, then refracts again on exit. Because n depends on λ, each colour leaves at a slightly different angle — red near 42.4°, violet near 40.6° from the anti-solar direction.

Why colours separate

The deviation of the emerging ray has a minimum (the "rainbow angle"). Light piles up near that minimum, giving a bright arc. Its exact value shifts with wavelength, so the arc splits into colours.

Total deviation: D = 180° + 2i − 4r, where sin i = n·sin r.
Primary bow angle: 180° − Dᵕᵢₙ ≈ 42° (red) → 40° (violet).

Scattering — Blue Sky & Red Sunset

Rayleigh scattering by air molecules is proportional to 1/λ⁴. Blue (short λ) scatters roughly 5–10× more than red, so the daytime sky looks blue. At sunset light travels a long slanted path; blue is scattered away and the transmitted beam looks red-orange.
