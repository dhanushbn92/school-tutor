Recombinant DNA Technology: Making Human Insulin

Follow the five steps used to make a genetically engineered bacterium that produces human insulin.

Donor DNA (human) Insulin gene Plasmid vector
  1. 1. Isolate DNA - extract the insulin gene from a human cell.
  2. 2. Cut - a restriction enzyme (EcoRI) cuts at its recognition site, leaving sticky ends.
  3. 3. Insert - cut the plasmid with the same enzyme; DNA ligase joins the gene into the vector.
  4. 4. Transform - the recombinant plasmid is taken up by a host bacterium (E. coli).
  5. 5. Express & multiply - bacteria divide and the gene is expressed, producing insulin.
Press Next Step to begin building a recombinant plasmid.
A restriction enzyme recognises a specific palindromic sequence (EcoRI cuts G↓AATTC) and leaves single-stranded "sticky ends" that base-pair with any DNA cut by the same enzyme. DNA ligase seals the sugar-phosphate backbone.

Tools & Techniques

Polymerase Chain Reaction amplifies DNA. Each thermal cycle has three steps and doubles the number of copies.

Ready. Run one cycle to see denaturation, annealing and extension.
Cycle: 0   Copies of target: 1
Denaturation (~94°C): strands separate. Annealing (~55°C): primers bind the template. Extension (~72°C): heat-stable Taq polymerase adds nucleotides. n cycles give up to 2n copies.

Gel electrophoresis separates DNA fragments by size. An electric field pulls the negatively charged DNA toward the positive electrode; smaller fragments move faster/farther.

Turn on the power to run the DNA fragments through the gel.
Lane loaded with fragments of 3000, 1500, 800 and 300 base pairs. The DNA ladder (left) gives reference sizes. Bands are visualised with a dye under UV light.

Facts Panel

  • Insulin: Recombinant human insulin (Humulin, 1982) was the first rDNA drug - safer than animal insulin.
  • Vaccines: Hepatitis B vaccine uses a surface antigen made by engineered yeast (a subunit/recombinant vaccine).
  • Gene therapy: A correct gene is delivered (often by a viral vector) to treat inherited disorders such as SCID (ADA deficiency).
  • Bt cotton: Carries the cry gene from Bacillus thuringiensis, which makes a protein toxic to bollworm larvae but safe for humans.
  • Golden Rice: Engineered to make beta-carotene (pro-vitamin A) in the grain.
  • Industry: Engineered microbes make enzymes, antibiotics, organic acids and biofuels.
  • Biosafety: India's GEAC regulates GM organisms; concerns include gene flow, biodiversity and ethics.
