What does "concurrent" mean?

Three or more lines are concurrent when they all pass through one common point, called the point of concurrency. Two lines usually cross at a point, but a third line need not pass through it — so concurrency of three lines is a special property. In every triangle the three medians, the three altitudes, the three perpendicular bisectors, and the three angle bisectors are each concurrent.

Lines in a triangle

Median

Joins a vertex to the mid-point of the opposite side. Three medians meet at the centroid, which divides each median in the ratio 2:1 from the vertex.

Altitude

The perpendicular from a vertex to the opposite side (or its extension). Three altitudes meet at the orthocentre.

Perpendicular bisector

The line perpendicular to a side through its mid-point. Three of them meet at the circumcentre — the centre of the circle through all three vertices.

Angle bisector

Divides an angle of the triangle into two equal parts. Three of them meet at the incentre — the centre of the inscribed circle.

The four centres at a glance

Centroid (G)

Meeting point of the medians. Always inside. It is the triangle's centre of mass and splits medians 2:1.

Orthocentre (H)

Meeting point of the altitudes. Inside an acute triangle, outside an obtuse one, at the right-angle vertex of a right triangle.

Circumcentre (O)

Meeting point of the perpendicular bisectors. Equidistant from the three vertices (distance = circumradius R).

Incentre (I)

Meeting point of the angle bisectors. Always inside and equidistant from the three sides (distance = inradius r).

The Euler line

In any triangle the orthocentre H, centroid G and circumcentre O are collinear — they lie on one straight line called the Euler line. Moreover G divides it so that HG : GO = 2 : 1. (The incentre is generally not on this line, and for an equilateral triangle all four centres coincide.)
