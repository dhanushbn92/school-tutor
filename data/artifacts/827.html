Innate immunity

  • Present from birth, non-specific.
  • Skin, mucus, stomach acid, fever.
  • WBCs engulf germs (phagocytosis).
  • Acts fast, no memory.

Acquired immunity

  • Develops after exposure, specific.
  • Lymphocytes make antibodies.
  • Memory cells remember the germ.
  • Vaccines build it safely.

Hygiene Tips

Personal hygiene

  • Wash hands with soap before meals & after toilet.
  • Brush teeth twice; bathe daily.
  • Trim nails; wear clean clothes.
  • Cover mouth when coughing/sneezing.

Public / community hygiene

  • Safe drinking water & proper drainage.
  • Dispose garbage; control mosquitoes.
  • Clean, ventilated homes & toilets.
  • Vaccination drives & awareness.

Pathogens

Pathogens are disease-causing microbes: bacteria (TB, cholera, typhoid), viruses (common cold, influenza, COVID-19), protozoa (malaria, amoebiasis), fungi (ringworm), and worms/helminths (tapeworm). They enter through air, water, food, contact or vectors like mosquitoes.

Communicable vs non-communicable

Communicable (infectious) diseases spread from person to person or via air, water, food or vectors — e.g. TB, malaria, cold. Non-communicable (non-infectious) diseases do not spread; they arise from lifestyle, genetics or deficiency — e.g. diabetes, hypertension, scurvy.

Immunity

Immunity is the body's ability to resist disease. Innate immunity is inborn and non-specific (skin, WBCs, fever). Acquired immunity develops after exposure and is specific: lymphocytes make antibodies and memory cells. Vaccines use weakened/dead germs to train immunity safely.

Balanced diet

A balanced diet supplies all nutrients in the right amounts: carbohydrates (energy), proteins (growth & repair), fats (stored energy), vitamins & minerals (protection & regulation), fibre/roughage (digestion) and water. Lack of a nutrient causes deficiency diseases like scurvy (vitamin C) or rickets (vitamin D).
