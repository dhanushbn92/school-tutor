Consequences of Pollution

Est. global temperature rise vs 1850: — °C
Greenhouse gases (CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, CFCs, water vapour) trap outgoing infrared radiation. More CO₂ → stronger trapping → warmer surface. The pre-industrial baseline is ~280 ppm; today it is above 420 ppm.
Rain pH: —
SO₂ and NOₓ react with atmospheric water to form H₂SO₄ and HNO₃. Clean rain is mildly acidic (pH ≈ 5.6 from dissolved CO₂); below that it is acid rain, which leaches soil nutrients, damages leaves, acidifies lakes and corrodes marble monuments (CaCO₃) — e.g. the Taj Mahal.
Stratospheric ozone: —% of natural · UV reaching ground: —
In the stratosphere, chlorine freed from CFCs by UV light destroys ozone catalytically (one Cl atom breaks thousands of O₃ molecules). A thin ozone layer lets more harmful UV-B through → skin cancer, cataracts, crop damage. The Montreal Protocol (1987) phased out CFCs.
Dissolved O₂: — mg/L
Excess N and P over-fertilise water → explosive algal bloom. Algae die, bacteria decompose them and consume oxygen. The water becomes hypoxic (a dead zone) and fish suffocate. This nutrient-driven ageing of a water body is eutrophication.
Top predator load: — ppm
Fat-soluble, non-biodegradable toxins (DDT, mercury, PCBs) are not excreted. Their concentration multiplies ~10× at each higher trophic level — biomagnification. Fish-eating birds accumulate the most; DDT thins egg shells and crashed raptor populations, prompting its ban.
