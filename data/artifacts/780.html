A transverse wave (like a string) has particle motion perpendicular to travel. A longitudinal wave (like sound) moves particles along the direction of travel, forming compressions and rarefactions. In both, energy travels but each particle just oscillates about its rest position. Note v = fλ holds live.

Two identical waves add by the principle of superposition. In phase (Δφ = 0°) → constructive, amplitude doubles. Out of phase (Δφ = 180°) → destructive, they cancel.

A standing wave forms when a wave reflects and superposes with itself. Nodes (○) never move; antinodes swing most. String fixed at both ends of length L: fₙ = nv/2L, so overtones are integer multiples of the fundamental f₁.

Two tones of nearly equal frequency superpose. Their amplitude slowly rises and falls — the loud–soft–loud cycle is a beat. You hear one beat each time the envelope peaks, at rate f_beat = |f₁ − f₂|. Set f₁ = f₂ and the beats vanish.

Key formulas

v = f λ
Wave speed = frequency × wavelength. Also v = λ/T, since T = 1/f.
y = A sin(kx − ωt)
Travelling wave; k = 2π/λ (wave number), ω = 2πf (angular frequency).
fₙ = n v / 2L
Harmonics of a string fixed at both ends (n = 1, 2, 3…). n = 1 is the fundamental; L = length.
nodes = n + 1,  antinodes = n
For the nth harmonic on a string fixed at both ends. Adjacent node–node spacing = λ/2.
f_beat = | f₁ − f₂ |
Number of beats heard per second when two close frequencies superpose.

Quick quiz

Tap an answer to check it and reveal the explanation.

1. A wave has frequency 2 Hz and wavelength 3 m. Its speed is:

v = fλ = 2 × 3 = 6 m/s.

2. Two identical waves meet exactly out of phase (Δφ = 180°). The result is:

Crest meets trough, so displacements cancel — destructive interference.

3. A tuning fork of 256 Hz sounds with another and 4 beats/s are heard. The other fork's frequency could be:

f_beat = |f₁ − f₂| = 4, so the other fork is 256 ± 4 = 252 Hz or 260 Hz.
