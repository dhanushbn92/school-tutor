Heat added → temperature rise

Heat is energy transferred (joules, J). Temperature is how hot something is (°C or K). Add the same heat to different amounts / materials and watch ΔT change: ΔT = Q / (m·c).

Water needs the most heat for the same rise (high c) — that is why it heats and cools slowly and moderates climate.

Heating curve of water: ice → water → steam

Heat 1 kg of ice at a steady rate. At 0°C and 100°C the temperature stops rising — the heat becomes latent heat that breaks bonds and changes the state.

Per 1 kg: melting absorbs Lf=334 kJ at 0°C, boiling absorbs Lv=2260 kJ at 100°C — both with no temperature change. Boiling needs far more energy, so its plateau is much longer.

Thermal expansion & the bimetallic strip

Heating makes particles vibrate more, so solids and liquids expand. Different metals expand by different amounts — a bonded strip bends, the basis of a thermostat.

Key facts & formulae

  • Heat gained/lost: Q = m · c · ΔT — m in kg, c in J/kg°C, ΔT in °C. Q is in joules (J).
  • Specific heat capacity c = heat to raise 1 kg by 1°C. Water c = 4200 J/kg°C (very high).
  • Latent heat (no temperature change): Q = m · L. Water: Lfusion = 334 kJ/kg, Lvaporisation = 2260 kJ/kg.
  • Temperature scales: K = °C + 273.15; °C and K change by the same size step; 0 K is absolute zero.
  • Heat ≠ temperature: a spark (high temp, tiny mass) carries little heat; a bathtub of warm water (lower temp, huge mass) carries far more heat energy.
  • Linear expansion: ΔL = L₀ · α · ΔT. Solids expand least, liquids more, gases most.
