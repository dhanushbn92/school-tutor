Mendel's Three Laws

Tap a card to reveal a worked example.

1. Law of Dominance

In a pair of contrasting factors, one (dominant) masks the other (recessive) in the F₁ generation.

Cross a pure tall (TT) pea with a pure dwarf (tt). All F₁ offspring are Tt and look tall — the dwarf factor is hidden, not lost.

2. Law of Segregation

The two alleles of a gene separate during gamete formation; each gamete carries only one.

A Tt plant makes gametes that are ½ T and ½ t. Crossing Tt × Tt lets the hidden t pair up again, so dwarf reappears in F₂ at 3 tall : 1 dwarf.
Tap to toggle example

3. Independent Assortment

Alleles of different genes are distributed to gametes independently of one another.

A dihybrid cross RrYy × RrYy (Round/Yellow) gives four phenotypes in a 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 ratio — seed shape sorts independently of seed colour.

From Gene to DNA

Chain: Cell → Nucleus → Chromosome → Gene → DNA. A gene is a segment of DNA that codes for one trait; humans have 23 pairs of chromosomes.

Sex Determination (XX / XY)

Mother is XX (all eggs carry X). Father is XY (½ sperm X, ½ sperm Y). The father's sperm decides the child's sex.

2 : 2XX girls : XY boys → ~50 : 50
Because the mother always gives an X, the sex of the baby depends on whether the sperm carries X (girl) or Y (boy).

Key Terms

Gene
A unit of heredity; a segment of DNA on a chromosome that controls one trait.
Allele
An alternative form of a gene (e.g. T for tall, t for dwarf).
Genotype
The genetic make-up (allele pair), e.g. TT, Tt, tt.
Phenotype
The visible trait produced, e.g. tall or dwarf.
Dominant & Recessive
A dominant allele (capital) shows even with one copy; a recessive allele (small) shows only when both copies are recessive (tt).
Homozygous / Heterozygous
Same alleles (TT, tt) / different alleles (Tt).
Mendel
Gregor Mendel, the "Father of Genetics", worked out heredity using garden pea plants.
