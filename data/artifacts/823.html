Why carbon is special

A carbon atom has 4 electrons in its outer shell, so it is tetravalent — it forms four covalent bonds by sharing electrons. This lets carbon combine with H, O, N, S, halogens and, crucially, with other carbon atoms.

Methane (CH4): one carbon shares four single bonds with four hydrogens.

Catenation: carbon's self-linking ability. C–C bonds are strong and stable, so carbon builds long chains, branches and rings — the reason there are millions of carbon compounds.

Homologous series: a family of compounds with the same general formula, where each member differs from the next by a –CH2– unit (mass 14 u). They have similar chemical properties and a gradual change in physical properties.

Functional groups

A functional group is an atom or group of atoms that gives a compound its characteristic chemical properties.

Alcohol  —OH (hydroxyl)
Ethanol
C2H5OH

Present in alcoholic drinks; used as a solvent and fuel. Reacts with sodium releasing hydrogen gas.

Carboxylic acid  —COOH
Ethanoic acid
CH3COOH

Dilute solution is vinegar. A weak acid — turns blue litmus red and reacts with bases and carbonates.

Two important reactions

1. Combustion

Hydrocarbons burn in air/oxygen giving carbon dioxide, water and lots of heat & light.

CH4 + 2O2 → CO2 + 2H2O + heat
C2H5OH + 3O2 → 2CO2 + 3H2O + heat

2. Esterification

An alcohol reacts with a carboxylic acid (with a little conc. H2SO4) to form a sweet-smelling ester and water.

CH3COOH + C2H5OH  →  CH3COOC2H5 + H2O

ethanoic acid + ethanol → ethyl ethanoate (an ester) + water

Esters smell pleasant and are used in perfumes and flavouring agents.

Key facts

Tetravalency: carbon has valency 4 — forms four covalent bonds.
Catenation: carbon bonds to other carbons forming chains, branches & rings.
Homologous series: a family with one general formula; members differ by –CH2–.
Functional group: the reactive part (–OH, –COOH) that decides chemical behaviour.
