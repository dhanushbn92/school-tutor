Punnett Square Builder

T = Tall (dominant), t = short (recessive). Try Tt × Tt for the classic 3:1.

R = Round, r = wrinkled · Y = Yellow, y = green. Try RrYy × RrYy for the 9:3:3:1.

How to read it

  • Each parent's alleles are split into gametes along the top and side (Law of Segregation).
  • Every inner cell combines one gamete from each parent — all equally likely.
  • The genotypic ratio counts genotypes; the phenotypic ratio groups them by visible trait.

Mendel's Three Laws

1. Law of Dominance

In a heterozygote, only the dominant allele is expressed; the recessive is masked. Cross of pure Tall (TT) × pure short (tt) gives an all-Tall F1 (Tt).

2. Law of Segregation (Law of Purity of Gametes)

The two alleles of a gene separate during gamete formation, so each gamete carries only one. When Tt forms gametes, half carry T and half carry t.

F1 Tt × Tt → F2 gives 3 Tall : 1 short (phenotype) and 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt (genotype).

3. Law of Independent Assortment

Alleles of different genes assort independently of one another (for genes on different chromosomes). A dihybrid RrYy × RrYy F2 gives the 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 phenotypic ratio.

Test Cross

To find whether a dominant-looking organism is TT or Tt, cross it with the homozygous recessive (tt).

If TT × tt → all Tall (100% Tt). Parent was homozygous.
If Tt × tt → 1 Tall : 1 short. Parent was heterozygous.

Key facts

  • Gregor Mendel worked on the garden pea Pisum sativum (1856–1863), studying 7 contrasting characters.
  • Alleles are alternative forms of a gene; homozygous = two identical, heterozygous = two different.
  • Genotype = genetic makeup; phenotype = observable trait.
  • Independent assortment holds only for genes on different chromosomes (or far apart); linked genes deviate.

Beyond Mendel & Chromosomal Inheritance

Incomplete Dominance

Heterozygote shows an intermediate phenotype. In snapdragon (Antirrhinum): Red (RR) × White (WW) → Pink (RW). F2 = 1 Red : 2 Pink : 1 White — phenotype ratio equals genotype ratio.

RR Red RW Pink WW White

Codominance — ABO Blood Groups

Alleles IA and IB are codominant (both expressed together); i is recessive. Pick parent blood genotypes to see offspring blood groups.

Sex Determination (XX / XY)

In humans, females are XX, males are XY. The mother always gives an X; the father gives X (→ girl) or Y (→ boy). So the father determines the sex, and the ratio is 1:1.

Sex-linked Inheritance & a Pedigree Reader

Genes on the X chromosome (e.g. red-green colour blindness, haemophilia) show criss-cross inheritance. Males (XcY) are affected with a single recessive allele; carrier females (XCXc) are usually unaffected.

Xᶜ (capital C) = normal dominant allele · Xᖸ (small c) = recessive affected allele.

Key facts

  • Blood group O (genotype ii) is the "universal donor" of the ABO system; AB is the universal recipient.
  • An AB × O cross can never produce an AB or O child — only A and B children.
  • Colour blindness affects ~8% of males but under 1% of females, because females need two recessive alleles.
  • An affected father can never pass an X-linked recessive to his sons (he gives them Y), but all his daughters become carriers.
