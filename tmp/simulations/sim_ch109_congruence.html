    Facts Panel

    SSS — Side-Side-Side
    If all three sides of one triangle equal the three sides of another, the triangles are congruent. Three fixed side-lengths lock the shape completely.
    SAS — Side-Angle-Side
    If two sides and the angle between them (the included angle) are equal, the triangles are congruent. The angle must sit between the two known sides.
    ASA — Angle-Side-Angle
    If two angles and the side between them are equal, the triangles are congruent. The side is the one joining the two known angles.
    AAS — Angle-Angle-Side
    If two angles and a non-included side are equal, the triangles are congruent. Because the third angle is fixed (angles sum to 180°), AAS reduces to ASA.
    RHS — Right angle-Hypotenuse-Side
    For right-angled triangles: if the hypotenuse and one other side are equal, they are congruent. This is the only criterion that starts from a right angle plus a non-included side.
    Why SSA is NOT valid
    Two sides and a non-included angle can produce two different triangles (the "ambiguous case"). The unfixed side can swing to two positions, so SSA does not guarantee congruence. Exception: when the angle is a right angle it becomes the valid RHS rule.
    Why AAA is NOT valid
    Equal angles guarantee the same shape (similar triangles) but not the same size — a small triangle and a large triangle can have identical angles. Congruence needs at least one pair of equal sides.
    CPCT
    Corresponding Parts of Congruent Triangles are Equal. Once two triangles are proved congruent by any criterion, all their remaining corresponding sides and angles are automatically equal. CPCT is used after congruence to prove further facts.
