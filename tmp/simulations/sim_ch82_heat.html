Fourier's law: the rate of heat flow is proportional to conductivity, area and temperature gradient, and inversely proportional to length. The colour band is the steady-state temperature gradient.

Convection — a fluid cell

warm fluid rises cool fluid sinks

Heated fluid at the base expands, becomes less dense and rises; at the top it loses heat, grows denser and sinks. This circulating loop is a convection current — the main way heat moves through fluids (and drives sea breezes and boiling water).

Radiated power grows as the fourth power of absolute temperature: double T and the emission rises 16×. Wien's law shows the peak wavelength shifts toward the blue as the body gets hotter (glowing red → white-hot).

Solar collector / greenhouse effect

A glass cover lets in short-wavelength sunlight but traps the long-wavelength infrared re-radiated by the warm absorber plate. Heat is held inside, so the collector runs hotter — the same trapping that warms a greenhouse.

Key formulas

Fourier conduction: Q/t = k·A·ΔT / L  — k = conductivity (W m⁻¹ K⁻¹), A = area (m²), L = length (m), ΔT = temp difference (K)
Stefan–Boltzmann: E = σ·ε·T⁴  — σ = 5.67×10⁻⁸ W m⁻² K⁻⁴, ε = emissivity, T = absolute temperature (K)
Wien's displacement: λmax·T = b  — b = 2.898×10⁻³ m·K (peak-wavelength constant)
