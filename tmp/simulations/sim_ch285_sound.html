Pitch & Loudness

Pitch: Medium
Loudness: Moderate
Band: Audible

Frequency → pitch (higher Hz = higher pitch). Amplitude → loudness (bigger swing = louder). Human audible range: 20 Hz to 20 000 Hz. Below 20 Hz = infrasound; above 20 kHz = ultrasound.

Echo, Reflection & SONAR

Round trip 2d = 200 m
Echo delay t = 2d/v = 0.583 s

A pulse travels to the wall and reflects back. From the delay: 2d = v × t, so distance d = v·t / 2. This is how SONAR finds sea depth and how medical ultrasound images the body — send a pulse, time the echo, compute distance.

Key Facts

v = fλ

Wave speed equals frequency times wavelength. In a fixed medium v is constant, so higher f means shorter λ.

Longitudinal wave

Sound travels as compressions and rarefactions; particles vibrate along the direction of travel. It needs a medium — no sound in vacuum.

Frequency = pitch

More vibrations per second = higher pitch. Measured in hertz (Hz).

Amplitude = loudness

Larger vibration = louder sound. Loudness level is measured in decibels (dB).

Echo

A reflected sound heard distinctly after the original. 2d = v·t. Ear resolves an echo if the delay is at least about 0.1 s.

Audible range

20 Hz - 20 kHz for humans. Below = infrasound, above = ultrasound (used in SONAR & scans).

Quick Quiz

1. Which property of a sound wave determines its pitch?

Pitch depends on frequency — higher frequency gives a higher pitch.

2. Sound travels fastest in which medium?

Sound is fastest in solids like steel (~5960 m/s) because particles are tightly packed; slowest in gases.

3. A SONAR pulse returns after 0.4 s in water (v = 1500 m/s). How deep is the seabed?

d = v·t / 2 = 1500 × 0.4 / 2 = 300 m (the pulse covers the round trip 2d in 0.4 s).
