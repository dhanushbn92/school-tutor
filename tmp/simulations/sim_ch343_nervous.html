Tap a neuron part

The yellow pulse is an action potential travelling along the axon. In myelinated axons it jumps node to node (saltatory conduction). Use the sub-tabs to see the synapse and a reflex arc.

Key facts

  • Resting potential approx -70 mV; maintained by the Na+/K+ pump.
  • Depolarisation: Na+ rushes in; repolarisation: K+ moves out.
  • Impulse is one-way across a synapse (transmitter only on presynaptic side).
  • A reflex bypasses the brain, giving a fast automatic response.

Tap a region

The nervous system splits into the CNS (brain + spinal cord) and the PNS. The PNS has a somatic (voluntary) part and an autonomic part, which is further divided into sympathetic and parasympathetic branches that oppose each other.

Quick reference

  • Cerebrum - thinking, memory, voluntary action.
  • Cerebellum - balance, posture, coordination.
  • Medulla - heartbeat, breathing, reflexes.
  • Hypothalamus - links nervous & endocrine systems; controls the pituitary.
Tap a gland on the body map for its hormone(s) and effect.

Nervous control — FAST

Electrical impulses; acts in milliseconds; short-lived; targets specific muscles/glands via nerves.

Endocrine control — SLOW

Chemical hormones in blood; seconds to hours; longer-lasting; can affect many organs at once.

Tap a gland

Each gland is ductless and releases hormone(s) straight into the blood.

Feedback loop — blood sugar

  • Sugar HIGH → pancreas releases insulin → cells take up glucose → sugar falls.
  • Sugar LOW → pancreas releases glucagon → liver frees glucose → sugar rises.
  • This negative feedback keeps blood glucose near a set point.
