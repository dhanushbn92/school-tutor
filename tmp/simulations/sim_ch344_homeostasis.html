Osmoregulation (ADH)

When blood is too concentrated, the hypothalamus signals the pituitary to release ADH (vasopressin). The kidneys reabsorb more water, urine becomes concentrated, and blood water returns to normal — the same negative-feedback logic.

Key Facts

Homeostasis

Maintenance of a stable internal environment (temperature, pH, glucose, water, ions) despite external change — essential for enzymes and cells to function.

Negative feedback

The dominant control mode: a change triggers a response that opposes and reverses it, returning the variable to normal.

Set point

The target value the control system defends (e.g. core temp ≈ 37°C, glucose ≈ 90 mg/dL). Receptors detect deviations from it.

Insulin vs glucagon

Antagonistic pancreatic hormones. Insulin lowers glucose (uptake + glycogen storage); glucagon raises it (glycogen breakdown).

Hypothalamus

Master coordinator: thermostat for temperature, osmoreceptor centre for water balance, and driver of many endocrine responses.

Positive feedback

Rarer — amplifies a change (e.g. childbirth, blood clotting). Not used to keep variables steady.
