Key ideas

Sample space (S)
The set of all possible outcomes of a random experiment. n(S) is its size.
Event (E)
Any subset of the sample space. An outcome in E is called favourable. P(E) = n(E)/n(S).
Simple vs compound
A simple event has exactly one outcome; a compound event has more than one.
Mutually exclusive
Events A and B cannot happen together: A ∩ B = ∅ (no common outcome).
Exhaustive
Events together cover the whole sample space: their union = S.
Complementary
A′ (not A) = S − A. A and A′ are mutually exclusive and exhaustive; n(A)+n(A′)=n(S).
