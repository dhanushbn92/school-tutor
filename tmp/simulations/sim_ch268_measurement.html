Least-Count Error

A reading is only as precise as the instrument. The uncertainty equals its least count.

Key Facts

  • The SI system has 7 base units. All other (derived) units come from them.
  • Least count = smallest main-scale division ÷ number of divisions on vernier/circular scale
  • Vernier caliper: main scale in mm, 10 vernier divisions → LC = 1 mm ÷ 10 = 0.1 mm = 0.01 cm.
  • Screw gauge: pitch 1 mm, 100 circular divisions → LC = 1 mm ÷ 100 = 0.01 mm.
  • Total reading = Main-scale reading + (vernier/circular coincidence × least count).
  • The uncertainty of any measurement equals the instrument's least count.
  • Significant figures show how precise a measurement is; keep only the digits you actually measured.

1. A vernier caliper has 10 vernier divisions matching 9 mm of the main scale. Its least count is:

0.1 mm
1 mm
0.01 mm
0.5 mm
LC = 1 mm main division ÷ 10 vernier divisions = 0.1 mm (= 0.01 cm).

2. Which of these is an SI base unit?

newton
mole
litre
joule
The mole (amount of substance) is one of the 7 SI base units. Newton and joule are derived; litre is a non-SI unit of volume.

Leading zeros are not significant. The first two sig figs are 4 and 5; the next digit 6 rounds 5 up to 6 → 0.0046.
