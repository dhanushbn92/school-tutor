Key Facts

Right-hand thumb rule
Grip a straight wire with the right hand, thumb pointing along the current. The curled fingers show the direction of the circular magnetic field around the wire.
Field of a straight wire
Concentric circles around the wire. Field B ∝ I / r — stronger near the wire, and it reverses when the current reverses.
Solenoid = bar magnet
A current-carrying coil has a uniform field inside: B = μ₀ n I (n = turns per metre). One end acts as N, the other as S.
Electromagnet
A solenoid wound on a soft-iron core. Strength grows with current I and number of turns N; it switches off when the current stops.
Force on a conductor
F = B I L. A current in a magnetic field feels a force — this drives the electric motor.
Fleming's left-hand rule
Thumb = Force/motion, First finger = Field, seCond finger = Current. Used for the motor (force from a current).
Faraday's law & induction
A changing magnetic flux through a coil induces an EMF: EMF = −N ΔΦ/Δt. Move the magnet faster → bigger deflection.
Fleming's right-hand rule & Lenz
Used for the generator (current from motion). Lenz's law: the induced current opposes the change causing it.
