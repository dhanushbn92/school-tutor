Key facts

  • Acids release hydrogen ions (H⁺) in water; they taste sour, turn blue litmus red, and have pH < 7. Examples: HCl, H₂SO₄, citric acid.
  • Bases / alkalis release hydroxide ions (OH⁻) in water; they taste bitter, feel soapy, turn red litmus blue, and have pH > 7. Examples: NaOH, KOH, Ca(OH)₂.
  • pH scale runs 0–14. pH 7 is neutral (pure water). Lower pH = more acidic (more H⁺); higher pH = more basic (more OH⁻).
  • Indicators change colour with pH: litmus, methyl orange, phenolphthalein (pink in base, colourless in acid) and the multi-colour universal indicator.
  • Neutralization: an acid reacts with a base to give a salt + water, e.g. HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O. The pH moves toward 7.
  • Salts form from the acid’s negative ion and the base’s positive ion. Common salt is NaCl. Some salts are acidic, some basic, some neutral.
