Worked word problems

1. A bag has 5 red and 3 black balls. One ball is drawn at random. Find P(black).

n(S) = 5 + 3 = 8. Favourable (black) = 3. So P(black) = 3/8 = 0.375. Then P(not black) = 1 − 3/8 = 5/8.

2. A die is rolled once. Find the probability of getting a number greater than 4.

Outcomes > 4 are {5, 6}, so n(E) = 2 and n(S) = 6. P = 2/6 = 1/3 ≈ 0.333.

3. A card is drawn from a well-shuffled deck of 52. Find P(a king).

There are 4 kings, so P(king) = 4/52 = 1/13 ≈ 0.077.

Key facts

  • Definition (equally likely): P(E) = number of favourable outcomes n(E)total number of outcomes n(S)
  • Range: for any event E, 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1.
  • Impossible event: P = 0 (e.g. rolling a 7 on an ordinary die).
  • Sure / certain event: P = 1 (e.g. rolling a number from 1 to 6).
  • Complement: P(not E) = 1 − P(E). E and "not E" together cover all of S.
  • Sum rule: probabilities of all outcomes of an experiment add up to 1.
  • Theoretical vs experimental: theoretical P is computed from counting; experimental P = (favourable trials)/(total trials). By the law of large numbers, as trials increase, experimental P gets close to theoretical P.
