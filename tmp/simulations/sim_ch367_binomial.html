Row n holds the coefficients of (a+b)n. Each entry is the sum of the two above it (Pascal's rule). Symmetry: nCk = nCn−k — the row reads the same forwards and backwards (its mirror cell is outlined).

If n is even there is one middle term (Tn/2+1); if n is odd there are two.

Putting a = b = 1 gives the sum of all coefficients = 2n. The special case (1+x)n = ∑ nCk xk.

Binomial Theorem

For any positive integer n:

(a + b)n = ∑k=0n nCk an−k bk

= nC0an + nC1an−1b + … + nCnbn. There are (n+1) terms.

General term Tk+1

The (k+1)th term is Tk+1 = nCk an−k bk, for k = 0, 1, …, n. Use it to find any single term without writing the whole expansion.

Binomial coefficient nCk

nCk = n! / (k!(n−k)!). It is always a whole number. Symmetry: nCk = nCn−k. Also nC0 = nCn = 1.

Pascal's Triangle

Row n lists nC0 … nCn. Pascal's rule: nCk = n−1Ck−1 + n−1Ck — each entry is the sum of the two directly above it.

Useful results

Sum of coefficients (a=b=1): ∑ nCk = 2n. Alternating sum (a=1,b=−1): ∑ (−1)k nCk = 0. Middle term is the largest coefficient.
