Constraints & the feasible region

A linear constraint is an inequality such as 2x + y ≤ 10. Each one divides the plane into two half-planes; the constraint keeps the side that satisfies it.

The feasible region is the intersection of all half-planes — the set of every point satisfying all constraints simultaneously. With x≥0, y≥0 it sits in the first quadrant.

Corner-point (vertex) theorem

For a linear objective on a convex polygon feasible region, the optimum (max or min) always occurs at a corner point (vertex). So we:

  • find every vertex (intersection of boundary lines that lies in the region),
  • evaluate Z = ax + by at each vertex,
  • pick the largest value (maximise) or smallest (minimise).
Objective function

The quantity being optimised, e.g. profit Z = 3x + 4y. Lines Z = k are parallel; sliding them (the iso-profit line) until they last touch the region locates the optimum vertex.

Bounded / unbounded / infeasible
  • Bounded: region enclosed — both max & min exist.
  • Unbounded: region extends to infinity — a max (or min) may not exist. If it does, still at a vertex.
  • No feasible solution: constraints contradict — the region is empty.
