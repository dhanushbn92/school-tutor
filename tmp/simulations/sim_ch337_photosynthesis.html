Overall Reaction

6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O ──[ light + chlorophyll ]──▶
C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂
Water is the electron donor; the O₂ released comes from splitting of water (photolysis), not from CO₂.
  • Light reactions — on the thylakoid membranes. Convert light energy → chemical energy (ATP + NADPH); release O₂.
  • Dark reactions (Calvin cycle) — in the stroma. Use ATP + NADPH to fix CO₂ into glucose. "Dark" = does not need light directly.
  • Photosynthetic pigment: chlorophyll a (P680, P700) plus accessory pigments (chlorophyll b, carotenoids).

What is happening

  • Photolysis at PSII: 2H₂O → 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ + O₂. Water is split, releasing oxygen and replacing electrons lost by P680.
  • PSII (P680) absorbs light, energised e⁻ passes down the electron transport chain (plastoquinone → cytochrome b6f → plastocyanin), pumping H⁺ to build a proton gradient.
  • Photophosphorylation: H⁺ flows back through ATP synthase → ATP (non-cyclic).
  • PSI (P700) re-energises the e⁻, which reduces NADP⁺ → NADPH via ferredoxin & NADP reductase.
  • Net products powering the Calvin cycle: ATP + NADPH; by-product O₂.
Cyclic photophosphorylation uses PSI alone to make extra ATP (no NADPH, no O₂).

Calvin Cycle (stroma)

Carbon atom ATP / NADPH used

Three stages

  • 1. Carboxylation: CO₂ + RuBP (5C) → 2 × 3-PGA (3C), catalysed by RuBisCO — the most abundant enzyme on Earth.
  • 2. Reduction: 3-PGA is phosphorylated by ATP and reduced by NADPH to form G3P (triose phosphate).
  • 3. Regeneration: most G3P regenerates RuBP (using ATP); some G3P leaves to build glucose.
  • Six turns fix 6 CO₂ and yield one glucose (using 18 ATP + 12 NADPH).
C3 vs C4: In C3 plants CO₂ is fixed directly by RuBisCO (first product 3C 3-PGA). C4 plants (maize, sugarcane) first fix CO₂ into a 4C acid (oxaloacetate) via PEP carboxylase in mesophyll cells, then release CO₂ to RuBisCO in bundle-sheath cells — reducing wasteful photorespiration.

Limiting factor

Adjust the factors

Each factor can be limiting. The rate follows the factor in shortest supply (Blackman's law of limiting factors). Temperature acts through enzymes: rate rises to ~25–35 °C, then falls as enzymes (RuBisCO) denature.
  • Low light → light-limited (flat CO₂/temp won't help).
  • Saturating light → CO₂ often becomes limiting.
  • Very high temperature → rate drops sharply (denaturation).
