How it works: Short-wave sunlight passes through the atmosphere and warms the ground. The warm ground radiates long-wave (infrared) heat. Greenhouse gases — CO₂, methane (CH₄), water vapour, nitrous oxide — absorb and re-emit this heat, keeping Earth ~33 °C warmer than it would otherwise be. Burning fossil fuels adds extra CO₂, thickening this "blanket" and causing global warming: melting glaciers and polar ice, rising sea levels, and shifting weather.

Ozone depletion: The ozone (O₃) layer in the stratosphere absorbs harmful ultraviolet (UV) rays. CFCs (chlorofluorocarbons) from old refrigerators, air-conditioners and aerosol sprays release chlorine that destroys ozone, creating the seasonal "ozone hole" over Antarctica. The Montreal Protocol (1987) phased out CFCs and the layer is slowly healing. Note: ozone depletion is a separate problem from the greenhouse effect.

Forest: deforestation vs afforestation

Biodiversity loss: Habitat destruction, pollution, over-hunting and climate change push species toward extinction, weakening food chains and ecosystem services. Sustainable practices: renewable energy, rainwater harvesting, organic farming, protected reserves & national parks, car-pooling, and the 3 Rs help keep resources for future generations.

Facts Panel

Greenhouse gases

CO₂, methane (CH₄), nitrous oxide (N₂O) & water vapour trap heat.

CO₂ is the biggest human-caused contributor, mainly from burning coal, oil & gas.

Pre-industrial CO₂ ≈ 280 ppm; today ≈ 420 ppm.

Ozone layer

Ozone (O₃) sits in the stratosphere and blocks harmful UV rays.

CFCs destroy ozone; the Montreal Protocol banned them.

Ground-level ozone, however, is a pollutant in smog.

Pollution

Air: smoke & vehicle exhaust → smog, acid rain, lung disease.

Water: sewage & industrial waste → unsafe water, dead fish.

Soil: pesticides & plastics reduce fertility. Noise > 85 dB harms hearing.

Conservation

3 Rs: Reduce, Reuse, Recycle.

Afforestation and reserves protect biodiversity.

Renewable energy & rainwater harvesting are sustainable.
