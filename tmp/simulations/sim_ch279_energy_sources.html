Key Facts

  • Law of conservation of energy: energy is neither created nor destroyed, only transformed from one form to another. A power plant just converts one energy form into electrical energy.
  • Renewable sources (solar, wind, hydro, biomass, tidal, geothermal) are replenished by nature and will not run out on a human timescale.
  • Non-renewable sources (coal, petroleum, natural gas, nuclear fuel like uranium) took millions of years to form and exist in limited amounts.
  • Fossil fuels (coal, oil, natural gas) release carbon dioxide and other pollutants on burning, adding to the greenhouse effect and global warming.
  • Most conventional plants share one idea: heat or moving water/wind spins a turbine that drives a generator to make electricity.
  • No source is loss-free — some energy always escapes as heat, so no conversion is 100% efficient.
