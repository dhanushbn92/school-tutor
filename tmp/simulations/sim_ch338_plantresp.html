Aerobic Respiration — The Big Picture

Cells break down glucose to release chemical energy stored as ATP. In the presence of oxygen this happens completely, yielding water and carbon dioxide.

C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂  →  6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + energy (~36–38 ATP)

Where each stage happens in the cell

Cytoplasm — glycolysis Mitochondrial matrix — link reaction & Krebs Inner membrane — ETC / oxidative phosphorylation

The four stages at a glance

1. Glycolysis

Cytoplasm. Glucose → 2 pyruvate.
Net 2 ATP + 2 NADH. Anaerobic start.

2. Link Reaction

Matrix. Pyruvate → acetyl-CoA.
Releases 2 CO₂, makes 2 NADH.

3. Krebs Cycle

Matrix. 2 turns per glucose.
4 CO₂, 6 NADH, 2 FADH₂, 2 ATP.

4. ETC / Ox. Phos.

Inner membrane. NADH & FADH₂ oxidised.
O₂ is final e⁻ acceptor → H₂O. ~32–34 ATP.

Key idea Most ATP comes from the ETC, not from glucose splitting directly. Oxygen's job is to accept electrons at the very end so the chain keeps running.

Aerobic vs Anaerobic Respiration

Without oxygen the ETC stops, so pyruvate cannot be fully oxidised. Cells fall back on fermentation to regenerate NAD⁺ and keep glycolysis going — but with a tiny ATP yield.

Respiratory Quotient (RQ)

RQ = volume of CO₂ released ÷ volume of O₂ consumed. It reveals the respiratory substrate:

Carbohydrate

RQ = 1.0
e.g. glucose: 6 CO₂ / 6 O₂.

Fats

RQ ≈ 0.7
Fats need more O₂ per CO₂.

Proteins

RQ ≈ 0.8–0.9

Anaerobic / organic acids

RQ > 1, or ∞ when no O₂ is used (alcoholic fermentation).

Facts Panel

  • Glycolysis is universal — it happens in the cytoplasm of every living cell, with or without oxygen.
  • The link reaction and Krebs cycle occur in the mitochondrial matrix; the ETC sits on the inner mitochondrial membrane.
  • Oxygen is the final electron acceptor in the ETC and combines with electrons and protons to form water.
  • All 6 CO₂ from a glucose molecule come from the link reaction (2) and Krebs cycle (4) — none from glycolysis or the ETC.
  • Fermentation yields only 2 ATP because glucose is not fully oxidised; its purpose is to recycle NAD⁺.
  • In plants, seeds and roots in waterlogged soil may switch to alcoholic fermentation, producing ethanol.

Quick Quiz (3 questions)

1. In eukaryotic cells, glycolysis takes place in the:

Glycolysis is entirely cytoplasmic and does not require oxygen or mitochondria.

2. What is the role of oxygen in aerobic respiration?

O₂ accepts electrons at the end of the ETC and combines with H⁺ to form water, keeping the chain running.

3. Compared with aerobic respiration, alcoholic fermentation of one glucose molecule yields:

Fermentation stops at glycolysis-level yield (2 ATP). Yeast produces ethanol + CO₂; muscle produces lactic acid.
