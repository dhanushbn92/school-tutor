Monera — the bacteria

Prokaryotic, single-celled organisms with no true nucleus and no membrane-bound organelles. Tap any labelled part of the cell to learn its job.

Key facts

  • Cell type Prokaryotic (no nuclear membrane)
  • Genetic material Single circular DNA in the nucleoid; often plasmids too
  • Cell wall Made of peptidoglycan (murein)
  • Gram +/− Gram-positive keep the stain (thick wall); Gram-negative do not (thin wall)
  • Nutrition Autotrophic (photo/chemo) or heterotrophic (saprophytic/parasitic)
  • Reproduction Binary fission; genetic exchange by conjugation

Protoctista

Mostly aquatic, eukaryotic organisms — a true nucleus and membrane-bound organelles. They include animal-like protozoa and plant-like algae. Tap a part or a card.

Key facts

  • Cell type Eukaryotic (true nucleus present)
  • Body Mostly unicellular; some algae are colonial/multicellular
  • Movement Pseudopodia, cilia or flagella
  • Nutrition Holozoic, photosynthetic or mixotrophic
  • Habitat Water, moist soil, other organisms

Fungi

Eukaryotic organisms with cell walls made of chitin. Their body is a network of thread-like hyphae called mycelium. Tap a part or an example.

Key facts

  • Cell type Eukaryotic; cell wall of chitin
  • Body Hyphae forming a mycelium (yeast is unicellular)
  • Nutrition Heterotrophic — saprophytic (dead matter) or parasitic (on a host)
  • Storage Food stored as glycogen (not starch)
  • Reproduction Mainly by spores; also budding & fragmentation
