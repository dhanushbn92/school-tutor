Evidence for Evolution

Fossils

Preserved remains or impressions of ancient organisms in rock. They show life has changed over time.

Example: Archaeopteryx — a fossil with reptile teeth and tail plus bird feathers, linking reptiles and birds.

Homologous organs

Same basic structure, different functions — inherited from a common ancestor (shows divergent evolution).

Example: forelimbs of humans, bats and whales share the same bone pattern.

Analogous organs

Different structure but same function — evolved separately for a similar job (convergent evolution).

Example: wing of an insect vs wing of a bird — both fly, but are built differently.

Vestigial organs

Reduced, functionless remnants of organs that were useful in ancestors.

Example: vermiform appendix, wisdom teeth and ear muscles in humans.

Embryology

Early embryos of different vertebrates look remarkably alike, hinting at shared ancestry.

Example: fish, tortoise, chick and human embryos all show gill-like slits early on.

Connecting links

Living or fossil forms with features of two groups.

Example: Duck-billed platypus — a mammal that lays eggs, linking reptiles and mammals.

Natural Selection — the Peppered Moth

Moths rest on tree bark. Birds eat the moths they can see. Pick a tree colour, then run generations and watch which moth colour survives — the environment "selects" the favourable variation.

Key Facts

Evolution

The slow change in inherited characteristics of populations over many generations, giving rise to new species from earlier ones.

Natural selection

Nature favours variations that suit the environment; those individuals survive, reproduce, and pass on the traits. Proposed by Charles Darwin (1859, On the Origin of Species).

Charles Darwin

English naturalist whose voyage on HMS Beagle and study of Galapagos finches led to the theory of evolution by natural selection.

Origin of life

Life likely began in ancient oceans ~3.8 bya from simple molecules. Oparin and Haldane proposed the chemical-origin idea, tested by the Miller–Urey experiment.

Types of evidence

Fossils, homologous & analogous organs, vestigial organs, embryology and connecting links all point to common descent.

Acquired vs inherited

Lamarck suggested inheritance of acquired traits; Darwin showed only heritable variations are passed on and shaped by selection.
