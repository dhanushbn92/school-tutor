CHAPTER 2 — OCEANS AND CONTINENTS

Introduction

Look at a globe. The blue covers most of the surface; the green-and-brown patches are smaller. The Earth's surface is divided between WATER and LAND — and the big bodies of water are called OCEANS, while the big landmasses are called CONTINENTS. This chapter introduces the seven continents and the five oceans, their relative positions, and where India fits in this picture.


2.1 LAND AND WATER ON EARTH

About 71 % of the Earth's surface is COVERED BY WATER. Only about 29 % is LAND. So the Earth's surface, viewed from space, looks mostly blue.

The water is divided into the OCEANS (the largest bodies), and smaller bodies — SEAS, GULFS, BAYS, LAKES, RIVERS. The land is divided into seven CONTINENTS and many smaller islands.

Together the oceans form one continuous 'world ocean' (water flows between them), while the continents are separate landmasses (some are connected, some are islands).


2.2 THE SEVEN CONTINENTS

In order from largest to smallest by area:

1. ASIA — the largest continent. Home to about 60 % of the world's people. Includes India, China, Japan, Russia (most of it), Saudi Arabia and many more. The mighty Himalayas, the Gobi desert, and many great rivers (Ganga, Yangtze) are all here.

2. AFRICA — the second-largest continent. Home of the Sahara desert (the world's largest hot desert), the Nile (one of the longest rivers), and a great variety of wildlife. Egypt, Nigeria, South Africa, Kenya are some countries here.

3. NORTH AMERICA — the third continent. Includes Canada, the United States, Mexico and the Caribbean countries. Major features: the Rocky Mountains, the Mississippi River, the Great Lakes.

4. SOUTH AMERICA — the fourth. Home of the Amazon rainforest (the world's largest), the Andes mountains (the world's longest mountain range), Brazil, Argentina, Peru.

5. ANTARCTICA — the fifth-largest. Almost entirely covered in ice. The COLDEST continent. No countries; only research stations from many nations. The South Pole is here.

6. EUROPE — the sixth. Home of countries like France, Germany, the United Kingdom, Russia (the western part). The Alps and the Ural Mountains. Smaller than Asia but very densely populated.

7. AUSTRALIA — the smallest continent. Sometimes called Oceania (when the surrounding Pacific islands are included). Home of unique animals like kangaroos and koalas.

A useful memory order (largest to smallest): ASIA, AFRICA, NORTH AMERICA, SOUTH AMERICA, ANTARCTICA, EUROPE, AUSTRALIA. Asia is by far the biggest — it covers about 30 % of all land.


2.3 THE FIVE OCEANS

Modern geography recognises FIVE oceans:

1. PACIFIC OCEAN — the LARGEST and DEEPEST. Lies between Asia/Australia (west) and the Americas (east). Covers nearly one-third of the Earth's surface — bigger than all the continents put together.

2. ATLANTIC OCEAN — the second-largest. Lies between the Americas (west) and Europe/Africa (east). The shape is roughly like a giant 'S'. Important for shipping between the Americas and Europe.

3. INDIAN OCEAN — the third. Bordered by Africa to the west, Asia to the north, Australia to the east, and the Southern Ocean / Antarctica to the south. India sits at its northern edge.

4. SOUTHERN OCEAN (also called the ANTARCTIC OCEAN) — the fourth. Surrounds Antarctica. Officially recognised as a separate ocean since 2000; some older books still call this region the 'southern parts' of the other three oceans.

5. ARCTIC OCEAN — the smallest. Surrounds the North Pole; mostly covered with ice.

The five oceans are CONNECTED — water flows freely between them. Together they make one 'world ocean'.


2.4 MAJOR FEATURES OF EACH CONTINENT (briefly)

- ASIA: highest mountains (Himalayas), greatest population, most diverse climate.
- AFRICA: largest hot desert (Sahara), longest river (Nile), wildlife reserves.
- NORTH AMERICA: Rocky Mountains, Mississippi-Missouri river system, Great Lakes.
- SOUTH AMERICA: Andes, Amazon basin, Atacama desert.
- ANTARCTICA: ice cap, South Pole, no permanent human population.
- EUROPE: many small countries, Alps, Mediterranean coast, dense rail network.
- AUSTRALIA: Great Barrier Reef, outback (interior dry land), unique animals.


2.5 INDIA IN THE WORLD

India is in the continent of ASIA, in the southern part. It lies on the NORTHERN edge of the INDIAN OCEAN. The Tropic of Cancer (23½° N) passes through the middle of India, so most of India is in the TORRID HEAT ZONE. The Bay of Bengal is to the east of India, the Arabian Sea is to the west, and Sri Lanka is to the south. To the north stretches the great Himalayan mountain range, which separates India from China.

India's neighbouring countries include: Pakistan (west), China (north), Nepal and Bhutan (north), Myanmar and Bangladesh (east), Sri Lanka and Maldives (south, across water).


SUMMARY

- About 71 % of the Earth is covered by water; 29 % is land.
- Seven continents (largest to smallest): Asia, Africa, North America, South America, Antarctica, Europe, Australia.
- Five oceans (largest to smallest): Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Southern (Antarctic), Arctic.
- Asia is the biggest continent (~30 % of land); the Pacific is the biggest ocean (~30 % of Earth's surface).
- Antarctica is the coldest continent; the South Pole is here.
- India is in Asia, on the northern edge of the Indian Ocean.
- Tropic of Cancer passes through India; most of India is in the Torrid heat zone.
- The five oceans are connected — water flows between them.
