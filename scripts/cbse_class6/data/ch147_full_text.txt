CHAPTER 1 — A BOTTLE OF DEW

Theme of the unit: Fables and Folk Tales

Introduction

Stories from long ago — passed down by grandparents, told around fires, written by wise people — are called FABLES and FOLK TALES. Each one carries a small lesson, a moral. The lesson is hidden inside a story so that we remember it. "A Bottle of Dew" is one such story. As you read, watch for the moral and notice how the story-teller uses simple events and characters to teach something big.


THE STORY (in brief)

Long, long ago there lived a wise old king. He was kind, but he had become very thoughtful in his old age. One morning he gathered the people of his kingdom in the courtyard and held up a beautiful crystal bottle, sparkling in the early sun.

"This bottle holds nothing today," said the king. "I will give it — and a great reward — to whoever fills it with what is most precious in the world."

The people went home, puzzled. The next morning, they returned. Each had brought what they thought was most precious.

A goldsmith brought a tiny bag of gold dust. "Pour it in," he said. "Gold is the most precious thing in the world."

A poet brought a few words written on paper. "Words live forever. They are the most precious."

A farmer brought a single grain of rice. "Without rice, no one eats. This is the most precious."

A child stepped forward last, holding a small leaf. On the leaf, balanced delicately, was a single drop of MORNING DEW. "Look, your majesty," said the child. "It came from the morning. It came from nowhere. It will be gone in a moment. But while it is here, it is the most precious thing of all — because it is now."

The king smiled. He held up the leaf and let the dew drop slide gently into the crystal bottle.

"It is the child," said the king. "Gold can be stored, words can be remembered, rice can be saved. But a drop of dew belongs to one moment only. The moment we are in is the most precious gift. I will keep this bottle, with one drop inside it, to remind myself every morning."

And so the bottle of dew became famous in the kingdom. Whenever someone forgot to be thankful for the present moment, they were told: "Remember the bottle of dew."


VOCABULARY (key words)

- gathered: brought together in one place.
- courtyard: an open space inside or beside a building.
- crystal: a clear, transparent material like glass.
- precious: of great value; not common.
- goldsmith: a person who works with gold.
- grain: a single small seed of rice / wheat / barley.
- delicately: gently, carefully.
- balanced: held in such a way that it does not fall.
- dew: tiny water drops that form on grass and leaves in the early morning.
- thankful: feeling or showing gratitude.

These words appear in many stories — recognise them so you understand fables faster the next time.


COMPREHENSION POINTS

1. WHO are the main characters?
   The king and four villagers — a goldsmith, a poet, a farmer, and a child.

2. WHAT did the king want?
   The 'most precious thing in the world', placed in his crystal bottle.

3. WHY did each person bring what they did?
   The goldsmith valued gold (wealth). The poet valued words (lasting beauty). The farmer valued rice (survival). The child valued the present moment (a thing that exists only NOW).

4. WHAT was the moral of the story?
   The PRESENT MOMENT is more precious than any object — because it cannot be stored or repeated.

5. WHY did the king keep the bottle?
   So that he would remember every morning to be grateful for the day.


LANGUAGE FOCUS

Common nouns and proper nouns. A common noun names a class of things (king, child, leaf, courtyard). A proper noun names a particular person, place or thing (always begins with a capital letter — e.g. Aesop, Mumbai). The story has very few proper nouns — most names are common: king, child, courtyard.

Adjectives. Words that describe a noun: a WISE old king; a CRYSTAL bottle; a MORNING dew; a SINGLE grain; a TINY bag.

Verbs in the past tense. Stories are usually told in the past tense. Pick out: GATHERED, BROUGHT, STEPPED, SMILED, KEPT. Each is the past form of a verb (gather, bring, step, smile, keep).

Synonyms (words with similar meaning):
- precious — valuable, treasured
- thankful — grateful, appreciative
- delicately — gently, carefully

Antonyms (words with opposite meaning):
- precious ≠ worthless
- thankful ≠ ungrateful
- delicately ≠ roughly


THINK AND WRITE

The story teaches us about appreciating the present moment. After reading, try these:

- Make a list of FIVE small moments in your day that you might be grateful for.
- Write a sentence describing a 'precious' object in your home — and one describing a precious MOMENT (not an object).
- Imagine you are the child. What did you feel when the king smiled at you?

These prompts help you connect the story to your own life — that's the whole purpose of a fable.


SUMMARY

- A fable is a short story with a moral hidden inside it.
- "A Bottle of Dew" is about a wise king who asks people to bring 'the most precious thing in the world'.
- A goldsmith, a poet, a farmer and a child all bring different precious things.
- The child's offering — a single drop of dew — wins, because it shows that the PRESENT MOMENT is the most precious thing of all.
- The king keeps the bottle to remind himself to be grateful each morning.
- Useful vocabulary: precious, thankful, delicately, balanced, dew, courtyard.
- Common nouns vs proper nouns; adjectives describe nouns; past-tense verbs tell what happened.
- Connect the moral to your own life: appreciate the small, present moments.
